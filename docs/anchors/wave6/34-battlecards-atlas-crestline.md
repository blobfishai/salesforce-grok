## Battlecard A — Atlas Prime Analytics

### Snapshot

Research/analytics attacker. Collides with Global Research Portal Seat Pack ($450k/yr list, 13-product-catalog.md), typically quoting ~30% below list (about $315k/yr entry price). Weak in regulated products: no prime brokerage, no FX liquidity, no treasury settlement.

### Strengths

- Modern dashboard UI and self-serve trials; short evaluation cycles.
- Aggressive entry pricing with a land-and-expand seat model.

### Weaknesses

- Zero regulated-product coverage; consolidation on Atlas is impossible for any client running Prime Brokerage Onboarding, FX Liquidity Access Tier-1, or Treasury Settlement Suite.
- AMER-only hosting; EMEA data-residency reviews stall.
- The entry price excludes seat overages and API metering; effective year-2 cost routinely exceeds the quote.
- No ESG integration; our ESG Analytics Add-on ($180k/yr) runs inside the portal.

### Landmine questions

1. "What is the all-in year-2 price at 500 seats, including the overage tier and API metering?"
2. "Where is EMEA user data hosted, and under which certifications?"
3. "If we later consolidate execution products, which regulated products can Atlas carry?"

### Objection-response pairs

1. **"Atlas is 30% cheaper."** The ~$315k entry excludes overages and metering; our $450k pack is flat. Any discount stays within tier authority — do not chase Atlas to -30%.
2. **"Atlas's UI is more modern."** Concede polish; pivot to workflow: research plus ESG Analytics Add-on plus regulated products under one contract and one compliance review.
3. **"We only need research today."** Buying the Seat Pack standalone carries no obligation — but expansion later needs no new vendor onboarding, security review, or data migration.
4. **"Atlas offered a 6-month free pilot."** Free pilots defer cost, not remove it. Counter with a paid 90-day pilot with defined success criteria and full credit toward year 1.

### Pricing-pressure guidance

Hold the $450k anchor. Concede seat-band flexibility or a multi-year term with the standard 7% uplift capped before conceding rate. Beyond authority, route to Deal Desk.

### Win/loss talking points

- **Win — Riverside Partners (Gold, EMEA):** Atlas could not commit EMEA hosting; closed at an 8% discount inside Gold authority.
- **Loss — Northgate Associates (Silver, AMER):** price-first buyer; the 5% Silver ceiling left no room. Lesson: qualify budget posture at the SAL gate per 02-lead-scoring-policy.md — reject with reason no_budget rather than carrying an unwinnable price fight to Negotiation.

## Battlecard B — Crestline Financial Cloud

### Snapshot

Wealth-platform attacker. Collides with Wealth Advisory Platform ($640k/yr list). Strong UI and demo presence, especially in the Family Office segment. No treasury settlement capability and no institutional product line.

### Strengths

- Polished advisor and client-portal UI; wins feature-tour demos.
- Fast implementation claims and strong family-office marketing.

### Weaknesses

- No equivalent to Treasury Settlement Suite ($2.4M/yr); wealth clients with treasury operations need a second vendor and hold no single SLA.
- No institutional path if a family office scales into a mandate.
- Shallow compliance tooling; advisor-recommendation audit trails are not regulator-export ready.
- Per-advisor pricing crosses over our flat $640k near 34 advisors at their published $19k/advisor rate.

### Landmine questions

1. "When a client needs treasury settlement, which vendor does Crestline hand you to — and who holds the SLA?"
2. "Show the compliance audit trail for advisor recommendations; is it exportable for a regulator request?"
3. "What happens to pricing at 40 advisors? At 60?"

### Objection-response pairs

1. **"Crestline's UI is nicer."** Concede the demo; weigh operations: one MSA covers Wealth Advisory Platform plus Treasury Settlement Suite and the full institutional catalog.
2. **"Crestline is cloud-native and modern."** Ask for their settlement and compliance SLAs in writing — settlement is absent, not merely slower.
3. **"We're a wealth shop; we'll never need treasury settlement."** Growth says otherwise, and 1-year auto-renew contracts with 60-day notice windows make late switching costly; buy the platform with headroom.
4. **"Crestline is cheaper per advisor."** Flat $640k beats $19k/advisor from roughly 34 advisors up; model the client's 3-year advisor headcount plan before comparing quotes.

### Pricing-pressure guidance

Concede a phased seat ramp or ESG Analytics Add-on bundling at list before conceding rate. Same authority ladder as above; beyond it, Deal Desk → Compliance → Finance.

### Win/loss talking points

- **Win — Harbor Associates (Gold, AMER):** renewal defended after Crestline's pilot failed the compliance-export test; the 120-day renewal motion in 14-renewal-playbook.md started on time and left room to run the test.
- **Loss — Summit Operations (Silver, APAC):** lost on UI after a feature-tour demo. Lesson: run the 60-minute demo on the client's advisor workflow, not our feature list.

For both competitors, track contested win rates against the 25% opportunity→win target; contested deals enter Commit only under the conditions in 12-forecast-methodology.md.
